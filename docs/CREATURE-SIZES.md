# Creature proportions

The complete 28-design collection was reviewed together so small creatures do not dominate larger fish just because their drawings use more characters. The goal is a readable visual hierarchy across Pi, Mini, and Mid. It is a stylized ocean, not a literal real-world scale model: making a whale and a small reef fish share exact biological proportions would make the fish unreadable on the Mini.

Six outliers were adjusted. The moon jellyfish is now **6 × 4 characters**, down from 15 × 7, making its drawing box about 77% smaller in area. The crab, lobster, and mantis shrimp also have smaller outlines. The reef shark is about 15% longer and the humpback whale about 11% longer, with their heights preserved. The other drawings retain their existing shapes after review.

## Shared drawing sizes

These are the actual character-grid bounds used in all three editions, including padding. Fins, claws, and tentacles can make a silhouette tall or wide without representing a large body. The ordinary humpback whale is the largest regular creature drawing; the submarine is a vehicle. Surface visitors use separate artwork and positions, and are not part of this 28-design lineup.

## Displays and parade

Mini and Mid use a 6 × 8 logical pixel cell. Mid enlarges the entire 320 × 320 scene to its 480 × 480 display. Pi uses its 16-pixel monospaced font. Every edition uses the same directional drawings and therefore preserves the same relative character-grid proportions.

The 28 designs keep their original parade order. The Mini parade now takes 114 seconds, Mid takes 99 seconds, and Pi remains two minutes. New dimensions are included in the swimmer spacing, full-entry/full-exit checks, and bottom-creature placement rather than only changing the visible drawing.
